Travel and tourism is an interconnected value chain—from destination stewardship and inspiration to reservations, ground operations, guest service and post-trip feedback. Explore a practical taxonomy of the work and systems behind it.

How to read this map: these are practical business-function categories for research and navigation, not a claim that one official global standard defines exactly 30 tourism industries. Companies often span several categories, and software capabilities overlap. Confirm current features, regional availability and integrations with each provider.

END-TO-END LIFECYCLE

How categories connect.

Discover & inspire

Destination marketing, content, metasearch and customer research help travellers identify places and experiences.

Sell & coordinate

Agencies, OTAs, GDS, booking engines, payment providers and suppliers convert interest into confirmed arrangements.

Deliver & improve

Transport, accommodation, attractions, customer support, analytics and sustainability functions deliver and refine the experience.

WHO OWNS THE WORK?

Map teams to responsibilities.

Commercial teams

Sales, distribution, pricing, partnerships, marketing and revenue management focus on demand, conversion and yield.

Operations teams

Reservations, dispatch, guides, property teams, safety, supplier management and service recovery deliver the promised product.

Enabling teams

Finance, HR, legal, IT, data, cybersecurity, accessibility and sustainability enable compliant, resilient operations.

SOFTWARE ARCHITECTURE

Understand the core systems.

System of record

Reservation, property-management, customer and finance systems hold operational records and workflows.

Distribution & connectivity

APIs, channel managers, GDS, booking engines and supplier connections exchange availability, rates, content and booking status.

Intelligence & control

Analytics, identity, permissions, monitoring, customer support and governance help teams operate and improve the ecosystem.

RESEARCH METHOD

Validate each category with evidence.

  • Define the buyer, user, workflow and business size.
  • Identify real products and the legal/provider company.
  • Link to the official product page and documentation.
  • Record integrations, region, pricing model and evidence date.
  • Separate verified facts from assumptions or marketing claims.

Directory quality rule

A category is a map of work, not proof that every business needs every system. Recommend products only after checking fit, support, data handling, costs and actual operating requirements.
